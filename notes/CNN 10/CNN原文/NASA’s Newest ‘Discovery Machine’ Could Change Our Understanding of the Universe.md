129 years ago today, one of history's most famous inventors helped shape the future of motion pictures. On August 31st, 1897, [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison) received a patent for his motion picture camera, the [Kinetograph](https://www.britannica.com/technology/Kinetograph), predecessor to modern **projectors**. It was cutting-edge technology for its time, capturing a rapid sequence of images onto strips of [**celluloid**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celluloid) film.

**projector** [prǝˈdʒɛktɚ] n. 放映机；投影机

**celluloid** [ˈsɛljǝˌlɔɪd] n. 赛璐珞；明胶

And when those pictures were viewed rapidly, one after another, bam, your brain perceived motion. While Edison is credited with the patent, here is your history **knowledge bomb**. Edison didn't do it alone.

**knowledge bomb** 知识炸弹; 震撼弹

His assistant, [William Kennedy Laurie Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kennedy_Dickson), is credited with the bulk of the experimentation and development, a reminder that some of history's greatest individual inventions were actually team sports. The Kinetograph helped pave the way for the development of movie projectors, theaters, and the entertainment experience we know and love today. Somewhere Edison's probably like, I should have patented popcorn, too.

A new eye in the sky has just launched into the **cosmos**.To help us better understand the mysteries of space, the [Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Grace_Roman_Space_Telescope) blasted off Sunday from [Kennedy Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center) aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket. Over the next five years, the bus-sized telescope will seek out otherwise invisible and faint space objects.

**cosmos** [ˈkɑzmǝs] n.（有和谐体系的）宇宙

And liftoff. Go Falcon Heavy and go Roman Space Telescope. And liftoff.

Is basically cosmic **panorama** mode. Roman is able to survey the sky up to 1,000 times faster than the famous [Hubble telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope) across a field of view about a hundred times larger, and it's expected to help answer some of science's biggest questions about [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter) and [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy).

U.S. President Donald Trump has signed an executive order beginning the process of creating a new U.S. Space Academy.

Think Naval Academy with a lot more rocket science. The proposed Academy would recruit and train aspiring astronauts, physicists, engineers, and aerospace technicians, among others. The president announced the move in Houston, Texas, while presenting the Artemis 2 crew with the [Congressional Space Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Space_Medal_of_Honor).

He said the U.S. Space Academy would train personnel for the Space Force and NASA, while also supporting the civilian space industry like [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX) and [Blue Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin). Important **distinction**, the executive order creates a commission to figure out how to make the Academy happen.
