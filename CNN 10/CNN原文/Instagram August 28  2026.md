Hey, Coy. Yeah, this really could be a turning point in how young people are protected on social media. These are the kinds of changes that parents and advocates, and even some teens themselves, have been calling on the social media platforms to make.

Now, Meta agreed to this settlement midway through a trial where just four of those state attorneys general had been seeking as much as $1 trillion in damages. That would have been a huge number. This $18 billion settlement is still a really significant number.

Some of these state attorneys have pointed out that this is the largest settlement with a big tech company in history. That money will go towards helping the states fund youth mental health programs, online safety trainings. But even more important here, I think, is the changes that Meta has agreed to make to its platforms.

I'll walk you through some of those. Teens ages 13 to 17 will now have a two-hour daily time limit across Facebook and Instagram. Only parents will be able to change that.

These platforms are also implementing a night mode, so teens will be unable to access them from midnight to 6 a.m., a school mode, so teens will be getting fewer **notifications** throughout the day. Meta is turning off likes on teens' content and removing access to extreme beauty filters. And it will also send teens prompts every 15 minutes to make them think a little bit more about how much they're using these platforms.
