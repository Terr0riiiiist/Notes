129 years ago today, one of history's most famous inventors helped shape the future of motion pictures. On August 31st, 1897, [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison) received a patent for his motion picture camera, the [Kinetograph](https://www.britannica.com/technology/Kinetograph), predecessor to modern **projectors**. It was cutting-edge technology for its time, capturing a rapid sequence of images onto strips of [**celluloid**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celluloid) film.

**projector** [prǝˈdʒɛktɚ] n. 放映机；投影机

**celluloid** [ˈsɛljǝˌlɔɪd] n. 赛璐珞；明胶

And when those pictures were viewed rapidly, one after another, bam, your brain perceived motion. While Edison is credited with the patent, here is your history **knowledge bomb**. Edison didn't do it alone.

**knowledge bomb** 知识炸弹; 震撼弹

His assistant, [William Kennedy Laurie Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kennedy_Dickson), is credited with the bulk of the experimentation and development, a reminder that some of history's greatest individual inventions were actually team sports. The Kinetograph helped pave the way for the development of movie projectors, theaters, and the entertainment experience we know and love today.
